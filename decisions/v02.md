## 1. Motivation

v01 did not beat row 5 (winner T012, 0.797 vs 0.799; decisions/v01.md §6). Three causes
are addressed here: (1) the search was blind: 11 of 13 experiments did not depend on any
result, and only 2 of 15 steps reacted to evidence; (2) age and sex entered only linearly
or through one joint projection, so a non-linear age effect cannot be expressed, and row 5b
showed no metadata gain (−0.001 [−0.014, +0.011]); (3) the fused recipes had a tuning
option (size) the baselines lacked (Problem 2), so the comparison was not matched.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Candidates: recipe × age-and-sex encoding (28 candidates)

The 11 recipes of v01 are kept. Each is combined with an age-and-sex encoding
(`src/fusion/metadata.py`); the pair is one candidate. Inputs: age `a` (years) and sex
`s` (male = 1). All encodings are fitted on the training patients only.

**Linear families (early E1–E4, late L1–L4).** A learned embedding followed by a linear
model gives the same predictions as the raw input, so only non-learned encodings that add
non-linearity are useful here:

| Encoding | Columns of the age-and-sex block |
| --- | --- |
| `raw` (v01) | `a` standardised, `s` |
| `ple` | piecewise-linear encoding of age (Gorishniy et al., 2022): T = 8 bins with edges at the training-fold quantiles of age; component t = clip((a − e_{t−1}) / (e_t − e_{t−1}), 0, 1); plus `s`. 9 columns |

In early fusion the 9 columns form the age-and-sex block (block-scaled as one block); in
late fusion they are the input of the age-and-sex base model.

**Learned family (N1–N3): feature tokenizers.** Each produces d-dimensional tokens
(d = the recipe's size):

| Encoding | Tokens |
| --- | --- |
| `joint` (v01) | one token: `GELU(W·[a, s] + b)`, W ∈ ℝ^{d×2} |
| `ft` | FT-Transformer feature tokenizer (Gorishniy et al., 2021): age token `a·w_a + b_a`; sex token `E[s] + b_s`, `E` an embedding table of 2 × d. Two tokens |
| `plr` | periodic embedding of age (Gorishniy et al., 2022): `ReLU(W·[sin(2πc·a), cos(2πc·a)] + b)` with 16 learned frequencies c (initialised N(0, 1)); sex token as in `ft`. Two tokens |
| `ple` | piecewise-linear encoding of age (T = 8) → `ReLU(W·PLE(a) + b)`; sex token as in `ft`. Two tokens |

How the tokens enter the mixers: N1 gates over all streams (image streams + age-and-sex
tokens); N2 attends over all tokens, each with a learned type embedding; N3 uses the mean of
the age-and-sex tokens as `h_M` in `(A·h_img) ⊙ (B·h_M)`.

Candidate count: early 4 × 2 + late 4 × 2 + learned 3 × 4 = **28**, against a budget of 20, so
the agent must choose.

### 2.2 Two kinds of experiment

- **Screen:** the v01 Phase 1 values (C 0.01 for image or joint LR; C 1 for the
  age-and-sex LR and the stacker; weight decay 0.01; size 32).
- **Tuned:** every hyperparameter chosen by inner 5-fold CV on the training folds, never by
  the validation score: C or weight decay from {0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1} and, for
  recipes with a size, the size from {16, 32, 64} (joint grid). Late fusion: every base model
  and the stacker tuned by their own inner CV, as in v01.

Fixed (never tuned): PLE bins T = 8; PLR frequencies 16; network training (AdamW, learning
rate 10⁻³, full batch, 100 epochs, dropout 0.25, 3 seeds averaged).

### 2.3 The loop (`policy: v02`)

Families: early, late, learned. Default encodings: early `raw`, late `raw`, learned `ft`.
Simplicity order: family (early < late < learned), then recipe number, then encoding
(`raw` < `ple`; `joint` < `ft` < `plr` < `ple`), then screen < tuned.

1. **Open (3 experiments):** screen E1·raw, L1·raw, N1·ft. Decision record `open`.
2. **Explore (screen experiments, until 17 experiments are used or nothing is left):**
   - *Drop:* after every experiment, a family with at least 2 experiments is dropped if the
     best experiment overall clearly beats the family's best (wins in ≥ 90% of the paired
     resamples). Decision record `drop`.
   - *Leader:* among families still in play with untried candidates, the one whose best
     experiment has the highest AUROC. A change of leader is a decision record `leader`.
   - *Next candidate in the leader:* first its untried recipes at the default encoding (in
     simplicity order); then the untried encodings of its best recipe; then those of its
     second-best recipe; and so on.
3. **Tune (3 experiments):** decision record `tune_start`; then tune the 3 best screened
   candidates of the families in play (highest AUROC first, at most one per recipe, each
   with its best encoding).
4. **Select:** among all finished experiments (screened and tuned) of the families in play,
   the candidate *c* has the highest AUROC; the winner is the simplest experiment *c* does
   not clearly beat (wins ≥ 90% of resamples and balanced accuracy at most 0.02 lower).
   Decision record `final_selection`. The winner is row 6 and is frozen in `selected.yaml`.
5. **Stop:** after the selection, or at 20 experiments (selection is then made on what
   exists).

Budget: **20 experiments** (≤ 17 to open and explore, 3 to tune), the same as v01.

### 2.4 After the search (outside the budget)

- **Matched baselines (fixes Problem 2):** the winner's recipe, encoding and tuning
  procedure applied to: age and sex alone (row 1m); each image model alone (rows 2m, 3m,
  4m); the best of those image models plus age and sex (row 5m = row 5b). Every row of the
  comparison then shares the winner's classifier family and tuning budget (TASK.md line 162).
- **Metadata diagnostic:** the winner without the age-and-sex block, compared with the
  winner (paired gain and CI). Never a candidate.
- **Version comparison:** v02's winner against the current best version (v01's T012), with
  the leaderboard rule.

## 3. Pre-registration

- **H1 (search):** with the same budget, spending experiments where the evidence points
  finds a better winner than v01. *Prediction:* winner AUROC ≥ 0.805; at least 5 decision
  records that changed the plan.
- **H2 (age and sex):** a non-linear encoding of age lets age and sex contribute.
  *Prediction:* among the explored encodings, `ple` or `plr` beats `raw`/`joint` for the
  same recipe; the metadata diagnostic shows a positive gain, of about +0.005 to +0.02.
- **H3 (fair comparison):** matched rows 2m–5m are within ±0.01 of rows 2, 3, 5.
- **Success criterion (primary):** v02's winner clearly beats v01's winner (leaderboard
  rule). *Secondary:* winner beats row 5 in ≥ 90% of resamples; metadata-diagnostic CI
  excludes 0.
- **Falsified if:** v02's winner is not above v01's and no encoding beats `raw`/`joint`.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30 min cap each.

## 6. Analysis

- **H1 (search) not supported for the score, supported for the loop.** 6 decisions changed the
  plan (3 leader changes, 2 drops, the start of tuning), against 2 in v01. But the winner (0.799)
  is not better than v01's (0.797), and the selection rule, which falls back to the simplest
  candidate unless the best wins in ≥ 90% of resamples, chose L1:raw although L4:ple scored
  0.013 higher with better balanced accuracy.
- **H2 (encodings) not supported.** `ple` helped L4 (0.806 → 0.812; balanced accuracy 0.433 →
  0.464) but hurt L1 (0.799 → 0.789) and L2 (0.808 → 0.805). The network tokenizers were barely
  explored: the learned family was dropped after 2 experiments (N1:ft, N2:ft), before `plr`,
  `ple` or `joint` were tried. The drop rule cut the family whose encodings were the point.
- **H3 (matched rows) partly.** The matched single-model rows are close to rows 2–5 (UNI2-h
  0.805 vs 0.798; 5m 0.794 vs 0.799).
- **Age and sex dilute averaging.** In L1, adding the age-and-sex model lowers AUROC: U + V
  0.806 → U + V + M 0.799; UNI2-h 0.805 → UNI2-h + M 0.794. A 0.61-AUROC model gets one third
  of the vote. The one recipe that learns how much to trust each source (L4) is the one where
  `ple` age helped.
- **Combining the two image models adds almost nothing.** U + V (0.806) ≈ UNI2-h alone (0.805)
  under the same recipe; consistent with their 47.5% shared errors (v01).
- **Inner-CV tuning hurts, every time.** Tuned versions were worse in 3 of 3 cases here
  (L4:ple 0.812 → 0.755; L2 0.808 → 0.797; L1 0.799 → 0.794) and 1 of 2 in v01. In T013 the
  age-and-sex model picked C = 0.0001–0.001 in 6 of 8 folds (its inner CV on ~113 patients
  cannot see a weak signal and switches it off), and the stacker followed. The fixed C = 0.01
  untuned UNI2-h (0.805) is above the tuned row 2 (0.798).

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **Bottleneck.** Not the search (the loop worked) and not the family of image fusion (U ≈
   U + V). Two problems remain: (a) **how much evidence each source contributes**: equal
   averaging lets a weak source (age and sex, AUROC 0.61) dilute a strong one, and high-capacity
   weighting (stacking, 7 × 28 + 7 parameters, or per-base inner-CV tuning) is too noisy at
   n ≈ 142; (b) **selection noise** in every data-driven choice made on ~113-patient inner folds.
2. **Assumptions that look wrong.** (i) That age and sex are a source like the images, to be
   averaged with them. Under the plausible assumption that morphology and age/sex are
   conditionally independent given the subtype, Bayes' rule gives
   `p(y | x, m) ∝ p(y | x) · p(y | m) / p(y)`: age and sex act as a **patient-specific prior**
   that multiplies the image posterior, and a weak prior automatically has little effect
   because p(y | m) is nearly flat. (ii) That the two image models are independent experts:
   they share most errors, so multiplying their posteriors would double-count; they should be
   pooled into one image expert. (iii) That more tuning is better: at this n, fewer free
   parameters estimated from proper scoring rules are safer than argmax-AUROC selection.
3. **Directions.**
   - *Metadata as a calibrated Bayesian prior / logarithmic opinion pool* (product of experts:
     Hinton, 2002; product rule for combining classifiers: Kittler et al., 1998; log-linear
     pooling: Genest & Zidek, 1986). Few parameters (source weights) fitted by negative log
     likelihood on inner out-of-fold predictions. Attacks (a) directly; low variance.
   - *FiLM conditioning* (Perez et al., 2018): age and sex modulate the image features in a
     network. Attacks (a) but adds parameters; the learned family has not been competitive.
   - *Stable tuning* (one-standard-error rule, averaging over the C grid). Attacks (b) but not
     the fusion itself.
4. **Choice.** The Bayesian prior / log-linear pool: it follows from first principles, matches
   the observed failure (dilution by a weak source, redundancy between image models), and has
   2–10 parameters instead of 200.

## 8. Proposal for v03

Add a new fusion family, **log-linear pools** (metadata as a prior): the image models are pooled
into one image expert, age and sex form a prior expert, and the fused log-posterior is a
weighted sum of their log-probabilities, with the few weights fitted by class-weighted negative
log likelihood on inner out-of-fold predictions. Policy, budget, encodings and the other
families unchanged. Specified in `v03.md`.
