# v04: tile-level multiple-instance fusion with attention, age and sex as log-prior and modulator

Status: analysed 2026-11-02. Git tag `v04`. Config: `conf/versions/v04.yaml`.
Outputs: `artifacts/versions/v04/seed_0/agent/`. Models: U, V. Previous version: `v03`.

<!-- Sections 1–4 were written before the run and are not edited afterwards. -->

## 1. Motivation

v01–v03 tried 48 experiments of fusion rules on the same inputs, the mean-pooled slide vectors,
and all good candidates land at 0.80–0.81 AUROC, within 0.01 of UNI2-h alone under the same
classifier (0.805); the remaining differences are below what 162 patients resolve
(decisions/v03.md §6–7). The fusion rule is no longer the bottleneck; the representation is. A
uniform mean over ~10,000 tiles weights tumour tiles like normal lung and stroma, while the label
is the predominant pattern of the tumour; the weakest classes (Lepidic, Papillary; AUROC
0.65–0.66) are defined by tumour architecture.

## 2. Design

**One structural change:** a new family of candidates, **tile-level multiple-instance fusion**
(`mil`), recipes M1–M3, that pool tiles with learned attention instead of a uniform mean and
fuse the two models per tile. Everything else is unchanged from v03 (policy v02, budget 20,
the 36 existing candidates, encodings, protocol).

**Tile cache (label-free, built once).** For every cohort slide, a fixed random subset of
N = 1,024 of its kept tiles (seeded by the slide number; every slide has ≥ 2,029 tiles), with
the UNI2-h class token (1,536) and the Virchow2 class + patch-mean token (2,560) of the **same
tiles**. Stored as `artifacts/features/tiles/<model>.npy` (203 × 1,024 × D, float32;
git-ignored). Built by `scripts/08b_tile_cache.py` from the cached embeddings; no model is
re-run.

**Architecture** (per slide; tile features standardised with means and SDs of the training
slides' tiles; age and sex tokenized with `ft` or `ple` as in v02):

- tile representation `φ_t = Dropout(GELU(W·x_t))` ∈ ℝ^d;
- gated attention pooling (Ilse et al., 2018): `a_t = softmax_t(wᵀ(tanh(A·φ_t) ⊙ σ(B·φ_t)))`,
  `s = Σ_t a_t·φ_t`;
- age and sex as an **additive log-prior** (v03): `z = h_img(s) + h_M(mean of age-and-sex tokens)`,
  where `h_img`, `h_M` are linear maps to the 7 class scores; prediction `softmax(z)`.

| Recipe | Tile input x_t | Pooling | Age and sex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | `[u_t ; v_t]` (per-tile early fusion) | one gated attention | additive log-prior |
| M2 | `u_t` and `v_t` in separate branches | gated attention per model; `z = (h_U(s_U) + h_V(s_V))/2 + h_M(·)` | additive log-prior |
| M3 | `[u_t ; v_t]` | one gated attention on FiLM-modulated tiles `φ'_t = (1 + γ(m))⊙φ_t + β(m)` (Perez et al., 2018) | modulator of the tiles **and** additive log-prior |

Candidates: M1–M3 × {`ft`, `ple`} = 6 (42 in total). Family order: early < late < pool <
learned < mil. Opening: E1:raw, L1:raw, P1:raw, N1:ft, M1:ft (5 experiments).

**Training (fixed in the config):** AdamW, learning rate 10⁻³, full batch over the training
slides, 100 epochs; each epoch uses a random 256 of each slide's 1,024 cached tiles
(augmentation by tile resampling); prediction uses all 1,024; dropout 0.25; d = 32; class-weighted
cross-entropy; 3 training seeds averaged; fp32, TF32 off, deterministic; RTX 4090.
*Screen:* weight decay 0.01. *Tuned:* weight decay from {0.0001, …, 1} by inner 5-fold CV; d stays
32 (a joint d grid would exceed the 30-min cap).

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** attention pooling over tiles recovers tumour-specific signal that the uniform
  mean dilutes, raising the ceiling above ~0.81; tile-level fusion lets the two models'
  views be combined where they matter.
- **Predictions:** (1) at least one MIL candidate ≥ 0.82; (2) the largest per-class gains over
  row 5 are in Lepidic and Papillary; (3) matched rows: MIL on UNI2-h alone (+ age and sex)
  is also above the mean-pooled rows, so part of the gain is representation; MIL on U + V
  beats MIL on U alone.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best version (v01)
  and beats row 5 in ≥ 90% of resamples. *Secondary:* it also beats the matched 5m row.
- **Falsified if:** no MIL candidate exceeds 0.81, or MIL is dropped as clearly behind.
- **Risk stated in advance:** ~0.27 M parameters for M1 at n ≈ 142 bags; variance may dominate.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

## 4. Implementation

- `scripts/08b_tile_cache.py`, `src/embeddings/tile_cache.py`: build and load the cache.
- `src/fusion/blocks.py`: an `ID` block (cache row of each patient) that only MIL recipes read;
  other recipes iterate `blocks.features` (all blocks except `ID`), so their behaviour is
  unchanged.
- `src/fusion/mil.py`: `MILFusion` (M1–M3); `src/fusion/registry.py`: recipes M1–M3 (family
  `mil`); `src/fusion/candidates.py`: builder (tuned: weight decay by inner CV).
- `scripts/10_fusion_search.py`: adds the `ID` block when the config has `tile_cache`; matched
  rows keep it.
- `conf/versions/v04.yaml`. Tests: cache determinism and alignment of the two models' tiles;
  MIL valid and deterministic probabilities on a synthetic cache; recipes of other families give
  identical predictions with and without the `ID` block.
- Run: `scripts.08b_tile_cache` once; then `--seed 0 --dev --config conf/versions/v04.yaml`,
  `--matched`, `scripts.version_leaderboard`.

## 6. Analysis

- **Hypothesis supported: the representation was the bottleneck.** Every MIL candidate (0.850–
  0.858) beats every slide-vector candidate of v01–v04 (≤ 0.812), and beats row 5 by +0.05 with
  CIs excluding 0 (shares ≥ 99.9%). Fold SDs fell from ~0.05–0.06 to ~0.02–0.03. The largest
  per-class gains are in Papillary (+0.15) and Lepidic (+0.13), the architecture-defined
  classes, as predicted.
- **But the gain is the representation's, not the fusion's.** Under the same recipe, UNI2-h alone
  (0.853) equals the fused winner (0.854); U + V without age and sex is 0.857; UNI2-h + age and sex
  0.851. Fusing Virchow2 adds ≈ 0; age and sex add ≈ 0 (−0.003 by the diagnostic). Prediction 3
  ("MIL on U + V beats MIL on U") is not supported.
- **The models are complementary per class, but joint training loses it.** UNI2-h's MIL is far
  better on Cribriform (0.947 vs 0.877), Virchow2's on Papillary (0.752 vs 0.722); the per-tile
  concatenation (M1) ends between them (0.891, 0.759): one shared projection and one attention
  dilute each model's strengths.
- **FiLM modulation by age and sex (M3)** did not help (0.850–0.852).
- **Tuning neither helped nor hurt** here (0.855–0.858 tuned vs 0.854–0.856 screen).
- **Against the fixed baselines the goal is met** (row 5 and rows 2–3); against the fair, matched
  single-model rows it is not: within the MIL representation, fusion and age and sex do not yet add.

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **Bottleneck now: fusion within the new representation.** The signal is there per model and
   per class (complementary strengths), but a jointly trained network fed with concatenated tiles
   cannot exploit it at n ≈ 142: shared capacity is spent on the stronger model's features.
2. **Assumptions that look wrong.** (i) That fusing early (per tile) is better because it is
   "more information"; with few bags, it is more parameters and less specialisation. (ii) That a
   network will discover how much to trust each source; v03 showed that a 2–3-parameter pool
   fitted by NLL on inner predictions recovers source reliability stably.
3. **Directions.**
   - *Expert fusion of separately trained MIL models* (one per foundation model, each optimised for
     its own predictions) combined with the age-and-sex prior by the v03 log-linear pool, weights
     fitted on inner out-of-fold predictions. Literature: stacked generalisation (Wolpert, 1992),
     product rule (Kittler et al., 1998), log-linear pools (Genest & Zidek, 1986).
   - *Other tile-to-slide aggregators* (TransMIL, DSMIL, CLAM; standing directive): may raise the
     representation further, but each raises fused and single-model rows alike.
   - *Metadata tokens inside a transformer over tiles* (cross-attention): richer, more parameters.
4. **Choice.** Expert fusion with log-linear pooling: it attacks the observed failure (dilution of
   complementary experts) with a handful of parameters, and reuses the v03 finding.

## 8. Proposal for v05

A new family, **pooled MIL experts** (`mexp`): per outer fold, one gated-attention MIL expert per
foundation model (no age and sex) and the age-and-sex logistic expert; inner 5-fold out-of-fold
predictions of every expert; combined by the v03 pools (product, two weights, per-source weights,
per-source weights + class offsets). Specified in `v05.md`.
