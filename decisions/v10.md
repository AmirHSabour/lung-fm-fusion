# v10: age and sex as attention queries (cross-attention pooling over tiles)

Status: designed 2026-11-02. Git tag `v10` (after the run). Config: `conf/versions/v10.yaml`.
Outputs: `artifacts/versions/v10/seed_0/agent/`. Models: U, V, G. Previous version: `v09`
(current best on U, V, G: `v04_uvg`, M2:ft 0.851; highest AUROC: C1:raw 0.861).

<!-- Sections 1–4 were written before the run and are not edited afterwards. -->

## 1. Motivation

v04–v09 locate the whole tile-level gain in *which tiles count* (relevance weighting, v06), and
show that experts share their errors (v09: ensembles sit between their members). Age and sex have
entered only after pooling (the product: +0.003 to +0.005, won in 89–94% of resamples) or by
modulating tile features in one jointly trained network (v04 M3 FiLM). They have never chosen
which tiles an expert reads. The user's standing directive asks for age-and-sex tokens attending
the tiles (docs/loop.md).

## 2. Design

**One structural change: the tile→slide aggregator becomes cross-attention with queries, and the
age-and-sex tokens can be queries.** Per model m, tiles `h_t = Dropout(GELU(W x_t))` (d = 32).
Queries `Q` (n_q × d) attend the tiles with one multi-head attention layer (2 heads, pre-norm,
residual; Perceiver cross-attention block, Jaegle et al., 2021):
`O = Q + MHA(LN(Q), LN(h), LN(h))`, slide score `z = V · mean_q LN(O)_q`.

| Recipe | Family | Queries | Age and sex enter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | `xq` | one learned query (control) | the X1 product with the logistic age-and-sex expert (`raw`, `ple`) |
| Q2 | `xqm` | the learned query + the age and sex tokens of the v02 tokenizer (`ft`, `ple`) | *inside* each expert, as queries choosing tiles (and through the residual); experts combined by the product of experts, with no separate age-and-sex expert |

Q1 isolates the aggregator; Q2 vs Q1 isolates "age and sex as queries" against "age and sex as a
prior". Training as before (100 epochs, AdamW lr 1e-3, weight decay 0.01, dropout 0.25, 256 tiles
per step, 3 seeds; 1,024 tiles at prediction); fixed. Candidates: Q1 × {raw, ple}, Q2 × {ft, ple}
= 4 (70 in total); families … < ens < xq < xqm; opening adds Q1:raw and Q2:ft (15 openings);
both untunable. Everything else as v09.

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** relevance depends on the patient beyond the image (e.g. background lung changes
  with age and smoking history, which sex partly proxies), so letting age and sex choose the tiles
  carries more of their information than a prior after pooling.
- **Predictions:** (1) Q2 (best encoding) ≥ Q1 (best encoding) + 0.005; (2) post-hoc, Q2 beats
  the same experts without age and sex (Q1's experts alone, Q1's metadata diagnostic) in ≥ 90% of
  resamples; (3) the best
  of Q1/Q2 ≥ C1:raw (0.861).
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best on U, V, G
  (`v04_uvg`) by the leaderboard rule, and beats each of its matched single-model rows (post-hoc)
  in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** Q2 ≤ Q1 (age and sex as queries carry no more than as a prior).
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

## 6. Analysis

**Hypothesis falsified.** (1) Q2 ≥ Q1 + 0.005: no (0.846 / 0.849 vs 0.847). (2) Q2 vs the same
experts without age and sex: 44%, no. (3) ≥ 0.861: no. Falsifier met: age and sex as attention
queries carry no more than as a prior; here both carry ~0 (Q1's prior: 55%).

**Mechanism.** The metadata queries change single experts by ±0.01 in opposite directions
(Virchow2 +0.012, UNI2-h −0.011, GenBio-PathFM 0.000): a perturbation of the same size as the
differences between aggregators, not a consistent effect. Age and sex do not identify which tiles
matter.

**Aggregators trade classes.** Dot-product cross-attention (softmax over q·k) loses Lepidic
(0.61 vs 0.79 with gated attention) and gains the high-grade patterns (Solid 0.95–0.96,
Cribriform 0.93–0.95, Micropapillary 0.87–0.88; best so far). Lepidic is the pattern closest to
normal alveolar lung; a sharp query finds dense tumour (high-grade patterns) and misses extensive
low-grade growth. With 15 Lepidic and 12 Cribriform patients these per-class AUROCs have CIs of
roughly ±0.07, so this is a signal to follow, not a result.

**Ten versions on the metadata.** Age and sex were tried as raw and piecewise-linear columns,
learned tokens (FT, periodic, piecewise-linear, joint), a log-linear prior with fitted or fixed
weights, FiLM on tile features, and attention queries. Their gain is 0 to +0.005 in every form,
won in 89–94% of resamples only in the product form. The sources' information, not the method,
is the limit: alone they reach 0.61–0.63, and the experts' shared error is image-side.

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **What limits performance now?** Error shared by all experts on the low- and intermediate-grade
   patterns: Lepidic vs Acinar vs Papillary (v05–v10), with In situ and the high-grade patterns
   separated well by some aggregator. The seven classes are treated as unrelated, although they
   form the IASLC grade groups (In situ; Lepidic = low; Acinar, Papillary = intermediate; Solid,
   Micropapillary, Cribriform = high), and the confusions follow those groups.
2. **Assumptions that could be wrong.** That flat seven-way cross-entropy is the right objective
   for 142 slides and 12–36 slides per class: the grade groups have 36–57 slides each, so a
   group-level target gives every expert more examples for the distinction it gets wrong.
3. **Directions.**
   - *Hierarchical auxiliary target* (label structure; the WHO/IASLC pattern groups, built from the
     label only; docs/loop.md "Label structure"): a grade-group head (4 groups) on the same
     pooled representation, loss = CE(7) + λ·CE(groups). Few parameters (d × 4). Attacks the
     observed confusion with more examples per decision.
   - *Per-class choice of aggregator* (gated attention for Lepidic, cross-attention for high-grade):
     fitted per-class weights; variance risk (v05: fitted pools lost).
   - *DSMIL, TransMIL* (standing directive): further aggregators; v06–v10 show aggregators trade
     classes rather than raise the total.
4. **Choice.** The hierarchical auxiliary target: it is the one untested objective-level change,
   it targets exactly the confusions every expert shares, and it adds ~130 parameters.

## 8. Proposal for v11

Experts trained with an auxiliary grade-group head (In situ; Lepidic; Acinar + Papillary; Solid +
Micropapillary + Cribriform), loss = CE(7 classes) + 0.5 · CE(4 groups), class-weighted. H1:
gated-attention experts (X1 with the auxiliary head); H2: CLAM-SB experts (C1 with it). Both in the
product with the age-and-sex expert. Specified in `v11.md`.
