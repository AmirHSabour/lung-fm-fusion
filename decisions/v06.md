# v06: composition pooling (tile class distributions averaged over the slide), on U, V, G

## 1. Motivation

v04–v05 moved AUROC from 0.80 to 0.854–0.858 by tile-level attention pooling; every fusion form
on top (joint networks, FiLM, fitted pools, products) stays within ±0.005 of the single UNI2-h
expert (v05 §5). The remaining errors are Lepidic (recall 3/15), Acinar (10/24) and Papillary
(14/31), confused with each other, while Solid, Micropapillary and Cribriform are detected well
(0.86–0.93). The label is the *predominant* pattern: the one covering the largest share of the
tumour (WHO/IASLC). Gated attention averages embeddings with weights that let a few tiles decide,
which suits presence-defined classes, not a proportion argmax. v05 also showed that the
zero-parameter product of separately trained experts with the age-and-sex expert (X1) is the
best fusion form found.

## 2. Design

**One structural change: the tile→slide aggregator.** A new family `agg` (recipes A1, A2) of
separately trained tile-level experts whose slide prediction is a *mixture of tile class
distributions* instead of a classifier on an attention-pooled embedding. Everything else as v05.

Per foundation model m and tile t (standardised with the training tiles' statistics):
`h_t = Dropout(GELU(W x_t))` (d = 32), `q_t = softmax(V h_t)` (7 classes).

| Recipe | Slide distribution p | Extra parameters vs A1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 `comp` | `p = mean_t q_t` (uniform over the cached random tiles: an estimate of the class area shares) | — |
| A2 `wcomp` | `p = Σ_t a_t q_t`, `a = softmax_t(gated attention(h_t))` (Ilse et al., 2018): tiles weighted by learned relevance, e.g. tumour vs non-tumour | 2d² + 3d + 1 |

Training: class-weighted NLL of `log p` (the v04 MIL settings: 100 epochs, AdamW lr 1e-3, weight
decay 0.01, dropout 0.25, 256 random tiles per step, 3 seeds averaged; all 1,024 tiles at
prediction). Each expert sees one model's tiles and no age and sex. **Fusion: the X1 product**,
`z = mean_m log p_m + log p_M` with the class-weighted logistic age-and-sex expert (`raw` or
`ple`, C = 1); zero fitted weights.

References: instance-level MIL with mean pooling (mi-Net; Wang et al., 2018, *Revisiting
multiple instance neural networks*); instance- vs embedding-level MIL and gated attention
(Ilse et al., 2018); attention-weighted instance contributions (Additive MIL; Javed et al.,
2022). Product of experts with a class-balanced prior: v05 §6.

Candidates: A1, A2 × {`raw`, `ple`} = 4 (54 in total). Family order: early < late < pool <
learned < mil < mexp < agg. Opening adds A1:raw (7 openings). `agg` is untunable (as `mexp`:
tuning experts inside inner folds exceeds the cap). Expert predictions cached on disk as in v05.
Models: U, V, G (docs/loop.md); the comparison baseline on three models is `v04_uvg`.

Bookkeeping fix (no effect on results): the record's `device` field names the GPU for every
network family (v05 §9).

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** a slide prediction built as a mixture of tile class distributions matches the
  definition of the label (largest area share), so it separates the proportion-defined patterns
  (Lepidic, Acinar, Papillary) better than attention-pooled embeddings, without losing the
  presence-defined ones.
- **Predictions:** (1) the best `agg` candidate ≥ the best `mexp` candidate + 0.01 (same models,
  same run); (2) mean AUROC over Lepidic, Acinar, Papillary ≥ 0.02 above that `mexp` candidate;
  (3) Solid, Micropapillary, Cribriform each within 0.02 of it; (4) A2 ≥ A1 (relevance weighting
  removes non-tumour tiles from the shares); (5) post-hoc, the same candidate without age and
  sex is below it.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best on U, V, G
  (`v04_uvg`) by the leaderboard rule, and beats each of its matched single-model rows
  (post-hoc, `scripts/11_post_hoc.py`) in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** no `agg` candidate exceeds the best `mexp` candidate.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

  `aggregator ∈ {attention, comp, wcomp}` (default `attention`: v04–v05 unchanged); for `comp`
  and `wcomp` the network returns `log p` (valid logits for the cross-entropy).

- `scripts/10_fusion_search.py`: `agg` settings; `device` field fix.
- `conf/versions/v06.yaml`: v05 + `agg`, models U, V, G.
- Tests: composition probabilities are a mean of tile distributions (sum to 1); `wcomp` with
  constant attention equals `comp`; expert pool with `comp` returns valid probabilities;
  default aggregator reproduces v05 predictions on the synthetic cache.
- Run: `--seed 0 --dev --config conf/versions/v06.yaml`, `--matched`,
  `scripts.11_post_hoc --of <winner and best agg>`, `scripts.version_leaderboard`.

## 6. Analysis

**Hypothesis falsified.** (1) best `agg` ≥ best `mexp` + 0.01: no, A1 0.797 vs X1 0.858 (falsifier
met). (2) proportion-defined classes improve: no, A1 is worst exactly there (Lepidic 0.594,
Papillary 0.686). (3) presence-defined within 0.02: no for Cribriform (0.862 vs 0.914). (4) A2 ≥
A1: yes, by +0.056 (post-hoc). (5) not reached.

**Mechanism.** What separates 0.80 from 0.85 is not the space in which tiles are averaged
(embeddings or class distributions) but *whether tiles are weighted by a learned relevance*.
Uniform averages (mean-pooled linear baselines, A1) sit at ~0.80; every relevance-weighted
aggregator (gated attention on embeddings, A2 on distributions) sits at 0.83–0.85 per model.
A slide holds normal lung, stroma, inflammation and tumour; the area-share argument holds only
over tumour tiles, and with uniform weights the non-tumour majority (normal alveoli resemble
lepidic growth) dominates the shares, so Lepidic fails first. A2 removes most of that but stays
level with attention MIL: once tiles are weighted, the space of averaging does not matter.

**Fusion and age and sex on three models.** GenBio-PathFM adds nothing given UNI2-h (fitted
weight ≤ 0.05; joint networks get worse with it: M1 0.854 → 0.834). The product X1 gives the
same 0.858 as on two models. Age and sex now help in 92.5% of resamples (+0.005) in the product
form: the first metadata gain above the 90% line, but small.

**Feedback loop.** 3 of 5 decisions redirected the search. Waste: 1.5 h on tuned MIL (two time
outs, one finished below its screen); the agent never ran A2 although within-family differences
were the largest effect of the version (0.056).

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **What limits performance now?**
   - *Learning the relevance weights from 142 weak labels* (main). Relevance weighting is the
     entire gain; it is learned from the slide label alone, through one attention map shared by
     seven classes. Evidence: §6; fold sd 0.03; per-model experts at 0.83–0.85 despite three
     different encoders.
   - *Redundancy of the encoders.* Three models, one ceiling: their errors are shared (product
     +0.005).
   - *Signal in age and sex:* bounded at ~+0.005 (four versions).
   - *Search:* the opening rule hides within-family differences; tuned MIL never fits the cap.
2. **Assumptions that could be wrong.** (i) One relevance map for all classes: each pattern may
   need its own (Lepidic evidence lives in different tiles than Solid evidence). (ii) The slide
   label is the only usable supervision: the attention's own top and bottom tiles give
   tile-level pseudo-labels (CLAM). (iii) The opening's "simplest recipe per family" represents the
   family.
3. **Directions.**
   - *CLAM* (Lu et al., 2021, *Nat. Biomed. Eng.*): gated attention with one attention branch per
     class (multi-branch) and an instance-level clustering loss on the most and least attended
     tiles; designed for data-efficient weak supervision. Attacks the main bottleneck directly.
     Cost: as M1 per expert, + K small branches. Risk: low–moderate (few extra parameters).
   - *DSMIL* (Li et al., 2021): a max-scoring critical tile plus attention by distance to it.
     Attacks relevance learning; one critical tile per class is fragile with mixed patterns.
   - *TransMIL* (Shao et al., 2021): self-attention among tiles; ~10⁵–10⁶ parameters for 142
     slides; attacks context, for which there is no evidence.
   - *Metadata tokens attending tiles:* attacks the age-and-sex bound (~0.005).
4. **Choice.** CLAM experts in the X1 product: the only direction that adds supervision to the
   relevance weights (the observed bottleneck), with class-specific relevance (assumption i) and
   tile pseudo-labels (assumption ii), at the cost of a few thousand parameters.

## 8. Proposal for v07

A family `clam` (C1 single-branch, C2 multi-branch) of per-model CLAM experts trained with
the slide loss plus the instance clustering loss, combined with the age-and-sex expert by the X1
product. Housekeeping from §6: the opening runs every recipe of the new family, and `mil` is
untunable (all tuned MIL runs on three models timed out or finished below their screen).
Specified in `v07.md`.
