# v03: age and sex as a Bayesian prior — log-linear pooling of calibrated experts

Outputs: `artifacts/versions/v03/seed_0/agent/`. Models: U, V. Previous version: `v02`.

## 1. Motivation

v02 (decisions/v02.md §6) showed that (a) averaging the age-and-sex model with the image models
dilutes them (U + V 0.806 → U + V + M 0.799 under L1; UNI2-h 0.805 → 0.794); (b) the two image
models are redundant (U + V 0.806 ≈ UNI2-h 0.805); (c) the only recipe that weighs sources
(L4 stacking, ~200 parameters) gave the best AUROC (0.812 with `ple`) but its tuned version
collapsed (0.755), because data-driven choices on ~113-patient inner folds are noisy. What is
missing is a fusion rule that weighs each source by its evidence with very few parameters.

## 2. Design

**One structural change:** a new fusion family, **log-linear pools** (`pool`), recipes P1–P4.
Everything else is unchanged from v02: the v02 policy, the budget (20), the encodings (`raw`,
`ple` for this family), the other 28 candidates, the evaluation protocol.

**First principle.** If the slide morphology `x` and age and sex `m` are conditionally
independent given the subtype `y`, Bayes' rule gives

    log p(y | x, m) = log p(y | x) + log p(y | m) − log p(y) + const.

Age and sex act as a patient-specific **prior** that re-weights the image posterior; a weak
prior is nearly flat and therefore changes little, which is the behaviour averaging lacks. The
two image models are not independent experts (they share most errors), so they are pooled into
one image expert rather than multiplied.

**Experts** (each the baselines' class-weighted multinomial logistic regression; class-balanced
training makes the implied prior p(y) uniform, so the −log p(y) term is a constant):
- for each image model b: `ℓ_b = log p_b(y | x_b)`, C = 0.01 (screen);
- age and sex: `ℓ_M = log p_M(y | enc(m))`, C = 1 (screen), `enc` = `raw` or `ple`;
- image expert: `ℓ_I = mean_b ℓ_b`.

**Pools** (fused scores `z`, prediction `softmax(z)`):

| Recipe | z | Free parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | `ℓ_I + ℓ_M` (Bayes' rule, product of experts) | 0 |
| P2 | `a·ℓ_I + b·ℓ_M`, a, b ≥ 0 | 2 |
| P3 | `Σ_b w_b·ℓ_b + w_M·ℓ_M`, all w ≥ 0 | one per source (3 here) |
| P4 | P3 + class offsets `c_k` (prior / threshold correction) | 3 + 7 |

The weights (and offsets) are fitted by minimising the class-weighted negative log-likelihood
(a proper scoring rule, smoother and less noisy than arg-max AUROC) on the experts' inner
5-fold out-of-fold log-probabilities, as stacking does; weights bounded in [0, 10], offsets in
[−5, 5]; L-BFGS-B. In `tuned` mode the experts' C values are tuned by inner CV (as in late
fusion); the pool weights are always fitted.

Candidates added: P1–P4 × {`raw`, `ple`} = 8 (36 in total). Family simplicity order:
early < late < pool < learned. Opening: E1:raw, L1:raw, P1:raw, N1:ft (4 experiments).

References: Kittler et al., 1998 (product rule for combining classifiers); Hinton, 2002
(product of experts); Genest & Zidek, 1986 (logarithmic opinion pools).

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** weighing age and sex as a prior on a pooled image expert removes the dilution
  seen in v02 and lets the weak age-and-sex signal add evidence.
- **Predictions:** (1) P1:raw ≥ L1:raw (0.799) and ≥ U + V without age and sex (0.806);
  (2) the best pool recipe reaches ≥ 0.810; (3) for the winner, the metadata diagnostic (the
  same pool without age and sex) is lower than the winner, wins ≤ 30% of resamples against it;
  (4) fitted weights give age and sex a weight > 0 and the two image models similar weights.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best version (v01,
  0.797). *Secondary:* the winner beats row 5 in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** every pool recipe is ≤ 0.806 (no better than ignoring age and sex) or the
  metadata diagnostic is not below the winner.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30 min cap each.

## 6. Analysis

- **Prediction 1 supported, 2–4 not.** P1:raw (pure Bayes' rule, no fitted parameters) reached
  0.807, above L1:raw (0.799) and level with U + V without age and sex (0.806): treating age and
  sex as a prior removes the dilution. But no pool reached 0.810; the learned pools (0.802–0.803)
  are slightly *below* the parameter-free P1.
- **The weights are interpretable and stable.** Across P2–P4 the fitted weights are image 0.60–0.62
  and age-and-sex 0.41 (`raw`) or 0.12–0.15 (`ple`); in P3/P4 UNI2-h gets 0.61 and Virchow2
  0.05–0.06. The pool learned, from inner predictions alone, what v02's matched rows showed:
  Virchow2 is redundant given UNI2-h, and age and sex carry some evidence.
- **Balanced accuracy improved with class offsets.** P4 (with offsets) has the best balanced
  accuracy so far (0.484), confirming that the arg-max decision rule, not the ranking, limits
  balanced accuracy.
- **A ceiling of ~0.81.** Every reasonable fusion of the two mean-pooled slide vectors and age and
  sex lands at 0.80–0.81 AUROC (v01–v03, 48 experiments), within ~0.01 of UNI2-h alone under the
  same classifier (0.805). The fusion rule is no longer the bottleneck.
- **Tuning hurt again** (L4:ple 0.812 → 0.755; L2 0.808 → 0.797; P1 0.807 → 0.800).
- **The selection rule** keeps returning the simplest late recipe (L1:raw) because no candidate
  beats it in ≥ 90% of resamples: the differences between good candidates (≤ 0.013) are below the
  resolution of 162 patients.

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **Bottleneck: signal in the representation.** Three versions of fusion rules on the same
   inputs hit the same ceiling; the remaining variation is noise. What every candidate shares is the
   input: one **mean** over ~10,000 tiles per slide. The label is the slide's *predominant growth
   pattern*: a property of the tumour regions, which occupy only part of the slide (normal lung,
   stroma, inflammation and secondary patterns make up the rest). A uniform mean gives the
   diagnostic tumour tiles the same weight as everything else. The weakest classes (Lepidic and
   Papillary, AUROC 0.65–0.66) are the ones defined by tumour architecture rather than by bulk
   tissue composition.
2. **Assumptions that look wrong.** (i) That the slide vector is a sufficient summary; it is a
   fixed, label-blind average. (ii) That fusion must happen after pooling: UNI2-h and Virchow2 embed
   *the same tiles*, so they can be fused per tile, before pooling, letting the pooling use both
   views. (iii) That age and sex must act only on the final decision: they could also change *which
   tiles* matter (In situ patients are younger; In situ tumours look like lepidic growth).
3. **Directions.**
   - *Attention-based multiple-instance learning* (ABMIL, Ilse et al., 2018): learn which tiles
     matter; the standard for slide-level classification from tile features (e.g. CLAM, Lu et al.,
     2021). Attacks the bottleneck directly. Risk: more parameters at n ≈ 142.
   - *Composition features* (k-means prototypes, bag-of-words histograms): label-free, but the
     mean already encodes composition linearly, so little new signal.
   - *Metadata-conditioned attention* (FiLM, Perez et al., 2018, applied to the tile features
     before attention): age and sex steer which tiles are attended.
4. **Choice.** Tile-level fusion with attention pooling, age and sex as an additive log-prior (the
   v03 finding) and, as a variant, as a modulator of the attention.

## 8. Proposal for v04

A new family, **tile-level multiple-instance fusion** (`mil`): per-tile fusion of UNI2-h and
Virchow2 embeddings, gated attention pooling learned inside each training fold, age and sex as
an additive log-prior (feature-tokenized) and, in one recipe, as a FiLM modulator of the tile
features before attention. Everything else unchanged. Specified in `v04.md`.
