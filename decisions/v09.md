# v09: ensembles of experts (product over aggregators and models) with the age-and-sex prior

Status: designed 2026-11-02. Git tag `v09` (after the run). Config: `conf/versions/v09.yaml`.
Outputs: `artifacts/versions/v09/seed_0/agent/`. Models: U, V, G. Previous version: `v08`
(current best on U, V, G: `v04_uvg`, M2:ft 0.851; highest AUROC: v07/v08 C1:raw 0.861).

<!-- Sections 1–4 were written before the run and are not edited afterwards. -->

## 1. Motivation

Three versions in a row lost when an expert gained parameters or input width (v06 three-model
concatenation, v07 class-specific attention, v08 context tokens; fold sd up to 0.041), while the
zero-parameter product X1 was never worse than its parts. The learned experts are high-variance
estimators. Their errors differ by aggregator and model (v08 §5: X1 leads on Lepidic, C1 on
Acinar and Papillary, the Virchow2 CLAM expert on context reaches 0.860 alone). Averaging many
high-variance, partly decorrelated estimators is the standard remedy and adds no parameters.

## 2. Design

**One structural change: the image term of the product becomes an ensemble of expert types.** A
new family `ens`, recipes Z1 and Z2:

`z = (1/|E|) Σ_{(m, e) ∈ E} log p_{m,e} + log p_M`

where p_{m,e} is the expert of model m ∈ {U, V, G} and type e, trained exactly as before (each on
its own tiles, no age and sex), and p_M the age-and-sex expert (`raw` or `ple`, C = 1).

| Recipe | Expert types e | \|E\| |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Z1 | gated attention (X1's), CLAM-SB (C1's) | 6 |
| Z2 | Z1 + both on context tokens (K1's, K2's) | 12 |

Zero fitted parameters. The experts are the same networks (same cache keys) as X1, C1, K1, K2, so
the ensembles reuse the run's cached predictions. Candidates: Z1, Z2 × {`raw`, `ple`} = 4 (66 in
total); family order … < ctx < ens; opening adds Z1:raw and Z2:raw (13 openings); `ens` untunable.
Everything else as v08.

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** averaging the log-probabilities of differently built experts reduces variance
  more than it adds bias, so the ensemble beats every member and the best single-model ensemble.
- **Predictions:** (1) the best `ens` candidate ≥ C1:raw (0.861) + 0.005 and wins against it in
  ≥ 90% of resamples; (2) Z2 ≥ Z1 (more decorrelated members); (3) post-hoc, the best `ens`
  candidate beats its UNI2-h + age and sex row (same ensemble on UNI2-h only) in ≥ 90% of
  resamples; (4) post-hoc, it beats its own version without age and sex in ≥ 90%.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best on U, V, G
  (`v04_uvg`) by the leaderboard rule, and beats each of its matched single-model rows
  (post-hoc) in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** no `ens` candidate exceeds C1:raw.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

## 6. Analysis

**Hypothesis falsified.** (1) best `ens` ≥ C1 + 0.005: no (Z1 0.8606 < C1 0.8614; falsifier met).
(2) Z2 ≥ Z1: no. (3) Z1 vs its UNI2-h + age and sex row: 64%, not ≥ 90%. (4) Z1 vs no age and sex:
94%, yes.

**Mechanism.** An ensemble beats its members only if their errors are partly independent. Z1 sits
between its members in every class, and adding the context experts (Z2) lowers it toward them:
the experts' errors are almost the same. The variance that limits these models is therefore not
training noise *between* experts (which averaging would remove) but error shared by all of them:
the same 142 labels, the same patients, the same patterns misread. This corrects v08 §7: added
capacity loses because every expert overfits the same way, not because of seed noise.

**Age and sex.** In the product the metadata gain is now consistent: +0.003 to +0.005, won in
89–94% of resamples in v06, v07 and v09 (X1, C1, Z1). Its size is bounded by what age and sex
carry beyond the image (alone 0.61–0.63).

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **What limits performance now?** Error shared by every expert: either signal the 112-µm tiles
   do not hold, or label noise in the predominant pattern (moderate inter-observer agreement; see
   v08 §7). Neither is reduced by averaging, by wider inputs or by more parameters. The single lever
   that worked (v04, +0.055) is *which tiles count*; v06 showed relevance weighting is the gain.
2. **Assumptions that could be wrong.** That relevance is the same for every patient given the
   image. The age-and-sex prior enters only after pooling (product) or by scaling tile features
   (v04 FiLM, jointly trained, 0.850). It has never been allowed to choose which tiles to read in
   an expert, the one place where v04–v09 locate the gain.
3. **Directions.**
   - *Age and sex as attention queries* (cross-attention pooling with latent queries, Perceiver;
     Jaegle et al., 2021; the standing directive's "age-and-sex tokens attending to the tiles"):
     tiles are pooled by learned queries plus the learned age and sex tokens; the metadata steers
     relevance. Few parameters (d = 32). Directly tests the remaining metadata mechanism.
   - *Hierarchical label structure* (In situ vs invasive, then grade groups): shares strength
     across confused classes; does not address shared error from label noise.
   - *Transformer over tiles (TransMIL), DSMIL, prototype pooling* (standing directive): further
     aggregators; v06–v08 suggest aggregators within relevance weighting are equivalent.
4. **Choice.** Age and sex as attention queries, with the same cross-attention pooling without
   metadata queries as its control: it is the only untested way for the metadata to act where the
   gain lives, it is the project's standing directive, and it adds ~10³ parameters.

## 8. Proposal for v10

Cross-attention pooling experts (Perceiver-style, one layer): Q1 = one learned query attending the
tiles (control; the experts enter the product with the logistic age-and-sex expert, as X1); Q2 =
the learned query plus the learned age and sex tokens (FT-Transformer or piecewise-linear
tokenizer) as queries, so age and sex choose which tiles to read; the Q2 experts already contain
age and sex and are combined by the product without a separate age-and-sex expert. Specified in
`v10.md`.
