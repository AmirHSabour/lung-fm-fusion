# v05: separately trained tile-level experts, combined with the age-and-sex prior by log-linear pooling

Status: designed 2026-11-02. Git tag `v05` (after the run). Config: `conf/versions/v05.yaml`.
Outputs: `artifacts/versions/v05/seed_0/agent/`. Models: U, V. Previous version: `v04` (current best).

<!-- Sections 1–4 were written before the run and are not edited afterwards. -->

## 1. Motivation

v04 raised AUROC from ~0.80 to 0.854 with tile-level attention pooling, but the gain belongs to
the representation: under the same recipe, UNI2-h alone reaches 0.853 and age and sex add ≈ 0
(decisions/v04.md §6). The two models are complementary per class (MIL on UNI2-h: Cribriform
0.947, Papillary 0.722; on Virchow2: 0.877, 0.752), yet the jointly trained network on
concatenated tiles ends in between (0.891, 0.759): shared capacity dilutes each expert. v03 showed
that a log-linear pool with 2–3 weights fitted by NLL on inner predictions recovers source
reliability stably and treats age and sex as a prior without diluting the images.

## 2. Design

**One structural change:** a new family, **pooled MIL experts** (`mexp`), recipes X1–X4. Everything
else is unchanged from v04 (policy v02, budget 20, the 42 existing candidates, protocol).

**Experts** (trained inside each outer training fold):
- one tile-level MIL expert per foundation model: the v04 M1 network on that model's tiles only,
  without age and sex (gated attention, d = 32, 100 epochs, 256 tiles per step, weight decay 0.01,
  3 seeds averaged), so each expert is optimised for its own predictions;
- the age-and-sex expert: class-weighted logistic regression on `raw` or `ple` age and sex (C = 1).

**Pools** (as v03, with `ℓ` = expert log-probabilities, `ℓ_I` = mean over image experts):

| Recipe | Fused score z | Free parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X1 | `ℓ_I + ℓ_M` | 0 |
| X2 | `a·ℓ_I + b·ℓ_M` | 2 |
| X3 | `w_U·ℓ_U + w_V·ℓ_V + w_M·ℓ_M` | 3 |
| X4 | X3 + class offsets c_k | 3 + 7 |

Weights (and offsets) are fitted by class-weighted negative log-likelihood on the experts' inner
5-fold out-of-fold log-probabilities (L-BFGS-B, weights in [0, 10], offsets in [−5, 5]).
Candidates: X1–X4 × {`raw`, `ple`} = 8 (50 in total). Family order: early < late < pool < learned
< mil < mexp. Opening adds X1:raw (6 openings).

**Engineering (not a design choice).** The MIL experts are identical across the eight candidates
(same data, settings and seeds), so their predictions are cached on disk within a run
(`work/experts/`), keyed by model, training rows, predicted rows and settings. **No tuned mode for
`mexp`:** tuning each MIL expert inside the inner folds would cost ~1 hour per experiment (over the
30-minute cap), and v04 showed that tuning does not change MIL results (0.852–0.858 tuned vs
0.850–0.856 screen). The tuning step therefore picks among tunable recipes only (config
`untunable_families: [mexp]`); screened `mexp` experiments still compete in the final selection.

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** experts trained separately keep their per-class strengths, and a pool with a
  few fitted weights combines them and the age-and-sex prior by their evidence, so the fusion
  exceeds the best single expert.
- **Predictions:** (1) the best `mexp` candidate ≥ 0.865; (2) it beats the matched single-model
  row (MIL on UNI2-h alone) by ≥ 0.01; (3) fitted weights give both image experts weight > 0.2
  and age and sex weight > 0; (4) per class, the fused model keeps UNI2-h's Cribriform and
  Virchow2's Papillary strengths; (5) the metadata diagnostic (same pool without age and sex) is
  below the winner.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best (v04, 0.854) by the
  leaderboard rule **and** beats the matched 5m row (best single model + age and sex, same recipe)
  in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** no `mexp` candidate exceeds the matched UNI2-h-alone row.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

## 6. Analysis

**Hypothesis not supported.** Predictions: (1) best `mexp` ≥ 0.865: no (0.858). (2) ≥ 0.01
over the U-alone row: no (+0.005, 79%). (3) both image weights > 0.2: no, w_V ≈ 0.06; w_M > 0
holds with `raw`. (4) per-class strengths kept: Papillary yes (0.766 > 0.752), Cribriform no
(0.917 < 0.947). (5) metadata diagnostic below the winner: yes (0.855 < 0.858), but only in 83%
of resamples. Primary criterion not met. Not falsified (X1 > U alone), but the gain is not
resolvable.

**Mechanism.**
- *The product rule is the right form; fitted weights are not.* X1 (zero parameters) beat X2–X4
  (2–10 parameters). With class-balanced experts, `ℓ_I + ℓ_M` is the naive-Bayes product
  p(y | image) · p(y | age, sex) / p(y) with a uniform p(y): the correct combination if image
  and age and sex are conditionally independent given the class. Fitting weights on ~130
  inner out-of-fold predictions adds estimation noise and no measurable gain (X2 vs X1: 29%).
- *The MIL experts are over-confident.* Fitted image weights of ~0.3 mean the inner
  predictions are best explained after shrinking the experts' log-probabilities by ~3×.
  Over-confidence only rescales log-probabilities, so it barely moves AUROC, but it lets the
  image experts swamp age and sex in X1.
- *Virchow2 is nearly redundant given UNI2-h* in the inner fits (w_V ≈ 0.06), although it helps
  on Papillary. Its information is real but smaller than the noise in 130 patients.
- *Age and sex add +0.002 to +0.005 in every form tried* (v01–v05: concatenation, encodings,
  priors, FiLM, products). Alone they reach 0.625. This is consistent with weak conditional
  information, not with a bad combination method.

**Failure analysis.** The remaining errors are Lepidic (recall 3/15), Acinar (10/24) and
Papillary (14/31), confused with each other. These are the patterns whose distinction is a
question of *how much* of the tumour shows each architecture: the label is the predominant
pattern, the one with the largest area share (WHO/IASLC grading). Gated attention lets a few
tiles decide the slide, which suits "is pattern X present" (Solid, Micropapillary, Cribriform
are detected well) but not "which pattern covers most of the tumour".

**Feedback loop.** 3 of 5 decisions redirected the search (D002, D003, D004). Waste: the two
tuned MIL runs timed out (1 h). The same configurations finished in 9 min in v04; the slow-down
(MIL screens also ran 3–5× slower: M1:ft 141 s vs 30 s) coincided with the GenBio-PathFM
extraction using the CPUs (14 workers per shard), so the likely cause is CPU contention in tile
loading. The timed-out runs were not repeated (docs/loop.mdC).

## 7. Step back (first principles)

1. **What limits performance now?**
   - *Bias in the aggregator* (main). The label is an area-proportion argmax; attention pooling
     estimates a weighted embedding, not proportions. Evidence: errors concentrate on the
     proportion-defined classes (Lepidic, Acinar, Papillary); presence-defined classes are at
     0.86–0.96.
   - *Selection noise.* Fusion variants differ by ≤ 0.005 with CIs of ±0.01; 162 patients cannot
     separate them. Only changes worth ≥ 0.01–0.02 are visible.
   - *Signal in age and sex.* Bounded: alone 0.625; conditional gain ≈ 0.003 in every form.
   - *Variance.* Fitted pools lose to the zero-parameter product; tuning never helped MIL.
2. **Assumptions that could be wrong.** (i) That one attention distribution can serve all seven
   classes: test with class-wise pooling. (ii) That a slide embedding is the right intermediate:
   the label is defined on tile-level patterns, so tile-level class scores may be the right
   intermediate. (iii) That age and sex should enter the image network: the product rule
   outside the network is as good as anything tried.
3. **Directions.**
   - *Composition pooling (instance-level MIL, mean of tile class probabilities).* Each tile gets
     a class distribution; the slide's distribution is their average over a uniform random
     sample of tiles, an estimate of the area fractions; the predicted class is its argmax. The
     inductive bias matches the label definition. Reference: mi-Net with mean pooling (Wang et
     al., 2018, "Revisiting multiple instance neural networks"); learning from label
     proportions. Cost: same as M1 per expert; no extra parameters. Risk: low.
   - *Additive MIL* (Javed et al., NeurIPS 2022): slide logits = attention-weighted sum of tile
     class contributions; between attention and composition. Same cost; low risk.
   - *Transformer aggregators (TransMIL, Shao et al., 2021) or DSMIL (Li et al., 2021):* model
     tile–tile interactions; ~10⁵–10⁶ parameters against 142 training slides; high variance
     risk; attacks a bottleneck (context) we have no evidence for.
   - *Metadata tokens attending tiles (cross-attention):* attacks the age-and-sex bottleneck,
     whose ceiling is ~0.003 by every measurement so far. Low expected value now.
4. **Choice.** Composition pooling, with Additive MIL as the second aggregator in the same
   family. It is the only direction that targets the observed failure (proportion-defined
   classes), costs no extra parameters, and keeps the X1 product with age and sex that v05
   showed to be the best fusion form.

## 8. Proposal for v06

v06 runs on **U, V, G** (GenBio-PathFM extraction finished; docs/loop.md). A new family of
tile-level experts with a different tile→slide aggregator, each expert trained on one model's
tiles without age and sex, combined with the age-and-sex expert by the zero-parameter product
(X1 form):
- *composition*: tile → class probabilities; slide = mean over tiles;
- *additive*: tile → class logits; slide = attention-weighted sum (Additive MIL).
Encodings `raw`, `ple`. Specified in `v06.md`.
