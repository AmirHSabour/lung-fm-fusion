## 1. Motivation

Across v05–v10 every expert, aggregator and fusion shares the same errors (v09: ensembles sit
between their members), concentrated on Lepidic, Acinar and Papillary. The seven classes are
learned as unrelated, from 12–36 slides each, although the patterns form the WHO/IASLC grade
groups (In situ; Lepidic: low; Acinar, Papillary: intermediate; Solid, Micropapillary,
Cribriform: high), and the confusions follow those groups. Age and sex add 0 to +0.005 in every
form tried (v10 §6); the remaining lever is the image experts' objective.

## 2. Design

**One structural change: the experts' training objective gains a grade-group head.** For an
expert with pooled slide feature `s` (gated attention: the attention-pooled feature; CLAM-SB: the
pooled feature of its single branch):
`L = CE_w(V s, y) + 0.5 · CE_w(U s, g(y))`, with g: In situ → 0; Lepidic → 1; Acinar, Papillary
→ 2; Micropapillary, Solid, Cribriform → 3 (built from the label only), `U` a new `Linear(d → 4)`,
both cross-entropies class-weighted ("balanced"). For CLAM-SB the instance loss is kept
(0.7 · slide + 0.3 · instance + 0.5 · group). The group head is used only in training; predictions
are the seven-class scores, as before.

| Recipe | Family | Experts (one per model, no age and sex) | Fusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | `hier` | gated attention (X1's) + group head | X1 product with the age-and-sex expert |
| H2 | `hier` | CLAM-SB (C1's) + group head | same |

λ = 0.5 fixed (not tuned). Candidates: H1, H2 × {raw, ple} = 4 (74 in total); family order … <
xqm < hier; opening adds H1:raw and H2:raw (17 openings); untunable. Everything else as v10.

## 3. Pre-registration

- **Hypothesis:** the group head gives every expert 36–57 slides per decision for the coarse
  distinctions it gets wrong (Lepidic vs intermediate vs high grade), which shapes the pooled
  feature (and, through it, the relevance weights) better than 12–36 slides per class.
- **Predictions:** (1) H1 ≥ X1:raw + 0.005 and H2 ≥ C1:raw + 0.005; (2) the largest per-class gains
  on Lepidic, Acinar, Papillary; (3) post-hoc, each H expert alone ≥ its counterpart alone for at
  least 2 of 3 models.
- **Success criterion (primary):** the winner clearly beats the current best on U, V, G
  (`v04_uvg`) by the leaderboard rule, and beats each of its matched single-model rows (post-hoc)
  in ≥ 90% of resamples.
- **Falsified if:** neither H1 > X1:raw nor H2 > C1:raw.
- **Budget:** 20 experiments; 30-minute cap each.

## 6. Analysis

**Hypothesis supported (weakly).** (1) H1 ≥ X1 + 0.005: yes (+0.0054, 96%); H2 ≥ C1 + 0.005: just
below (+0.004, 92%). (2) Gains on Lepidic/Acinar/Papillary: H1 vs X1 +0.002/+0.004/+0.010; H2 vs C1
+0.012/+0.003/+0.010; spread over all classes, not concentrated there. (3) Each group-head expert
alone ≥ its counterpart: U 0.856 vs 0.853 (attention), V 0.854 vs 0.841, G 0.841 vs 0.837 (3 of 3
for H1). Not falsified: the first version since v04 whose change beats its own control in ≥ 90% of
resamples for both recipes.

**Mechanism.** The grade-group head pools classes into groups of 36–57 slides and trains the
shared pooled feature on that coarser, less noisy target; every expert improves a little and the
product keeps the gains. Size: +0.002 to +0.008, near the resolution limit of 162 patients; the
global winner's curse is ~+0.004.
